Who runs a club

If you can gather students regularly, you can run one.

Clubs work best with a steady group and a regular meeting time. Beyond that, they can look very different from one place to the next.

🏫

Schools

An after-school or lunch club, led by a teacher who wants to keep the momentum going after a workshop.

📚

Libraries and makerspaces

A weekly or monthly meetup that brings hardware to students whose schools don't offer it.

🤝

Community organizations

Youth centers, Boys & Girls Clubs, scout troops, faith communities, and cultural organizations.

🙋

Individuals

Parents, homeschool groups, college students, and engineers who want to lead a group in their neighborhood.

What we provide

You don't start from a blank page.

We're packaging everything that makes our workshops work, so another leader can run a club well.

01

Leader training

A training session on the hardware, the curriculum, and how to run a room where students debug their own circuits.

02

Instructor manual

Session-by-session lesson plans, timing, talking points, and a troubleshooting guide for the wiring and code problems students hit most.

03

Full curriculum

Slides, student handouts, starter code, and project cards, from "what is a microcontroller" to designing and presenting an original build.

04

Kit parts list and sourcing

An exact bill of materials and where to buy it. Where donations allow, we help new clubs with starter kits.

05

Ongoing support

A direct line to our team for questions, plus new project ideas as the program grows.

06

Showcase and recognition

A showcase format and certificates, so your students get the same moment of presenting their work that workshop students do.

How it works

From application to first meeting.

1

Apply

Tell us who you are, where you'd meet, and roughly how many students you expect.

2

Talk it through

We have a short call to understand your setting and answer your questions.

3

Get trained

You complete leader training and receive the manual, curriculum, and parts list.

4

Launch

Hold your first meeting. We stay in touch and can visit to help run it.

What a club leader commits to

A short list.

  • ✓Complete leader training before the first meeting.
  • ✓Meet regularly, weekly or every other week, for at least one semester.
  • ✓Keep it free for students. Clubs using our materials don't charge families.
  • ✓Follow your host's safety rules, including background checks for adults working with minors.
  • ✓Share a short update each semester so we can improve the materials.

What a meeting needs

About the same as a workshop.

  • ✓A room with tables and outlets.
  • ✓Computers students can code on. Chromebooks work.
  • ✓Hardware kits. Shared between pairs is fine.
  • ✓60 to 90 minutes per meeting.
  • ✓No space? Our donors network may be able to help.
