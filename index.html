The program

Three sessions. Ten hours. A kit for every student.

Here is the whole thing: what gets taught, and what happens in each session.

Session 01

Fundamentals

What a computer actually is, then an introduction to microcontrollers, breadboards, and a set of sensors and motors. Everyone gets hands on the hardware in session one.

Session 02

Design and wire your own

Students design and wire their own project. Instructors are told to guide rather than solve, even when solving would be faster for everyone in the room.

Session 03

Showcase and awards

Students present what they made and explain how it works, in front of the room, to people who did not build it.

Why we exist

Most students never touch the hardware side of STEM.

School computing has largely settled on software: simple block, then Python, then maybe a robotics club with a fixed kit. But there's a whole other side of computing students rarely get to experience. The physical layer underneath, where a sensor reading triggers a decision and becomes a moving part, is where a lot of students would actually catch fire. It also needs equipment, a curriculum, and someone in the room who has wired a circuit before. That's why we're here.

Equipment is the first wall

Students don't often have access to microcontrollers, breadboards, sensors, and motors.

Then there's the knowledge gap

Most computer science certifications do not cover the hardware side of computing, so students often don't have the time to learn the material on their own.

So most students never get the chance

Students decide what they are good at long before they choose a major. If nobody puts a breadboard in front of a student, they're unlikely to know whether they would enjoy working with one.

Track record

We have run this before.

In 2021, our founder, Ronith Balusani, opened a Raspberry Pi summer program for his entire grade. Thirty students signed up and the program ran for seven weeks that summer, with families rearranging their schedules around it rather than dropping out.

About us

Built by people who wished this had existed.

STEM Begins Here is an Arizona nonprofit that puts real hardware in front of students in grades 6 to 12, because the gap between wanting to build something and knowing how to start is mostly a matter of access.

I started creating hardware engineering projects at 8 years old, but I found it very frustrating that there were no easy ways to access courses which gave a strong breakdown of the fundamentals of hardware engineering. I wanted to create a program that would give students the opportunity to learn about hardware engineering in a hands-on way, and to inspire them to solve problems in their own lives and across different fields with technology.

RB Ronith BalusaniFounder & Lead Instructor · teaching hardware since 2021
