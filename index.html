The program

One day or two. A kit for every student.

Pick the format that fits your calendar. Both cover the same foundation; two days gives students time to design their own project.

1-Day Workshop

The fast start

One day, start to finish. Students learn what a computer is, get hands on a microcontroller, breadboard, sensors, and motors, then wire and code a working project of their own before they leave. It ends with a short demo.

2-Day Workshop

Build your own

Day 1, fundamentals: computers, microcontrollers, breadboards, and a set of sensors and motors, with everyone hands-on from the start.

Day 2, design and showcase: students design and wire their own project, then present it and explain how it works. Instructors guide rather than solve. Awards to close.

Why we exist

Most students never touch the hardware side of STEM.

School computing has largely settled on software: simple block, then Python, then maybe a robotics club with a fixed kit. But there's a whole other side of computing students rarely get to experience. The physical layer underneath, where a sensor reading triggers a decision and becomes a moving part, is where a lot of students find what they love. It also needs equipment, a curriculum, and someone in the room who has wired a circuit before. That's why we're here.

Equipment is the first wall

Students don't often have access to microcontrollers, breadboards, sensors, and motors.

Then there's the knowledge gap

Most computer science classes don't cover the hardware side of computing, and learning it alone takes time and guidance most students don't have.

So most students never get the chance

Students decide what they are good at long before they choose a major. If nobody puts a breadboard in front of a student, they're unlikely to know whether they would enjoy working with one.

About us

Built by people who wished this had existed.

STEMBeginsHere is an Arizona nonprofit that puts real hardware in front of students in grades 6 to 12, because the gap between wanting to build something and knowing how to start is mostly a matter of access.

I started creating hardware engineering projects at 8 years old, but I found it very frustrating that there were no easy ways to access courses which gave a strong breakdown of the fundamentals of hardware engineering. I wanted to create a program that would give students the opportunity to learn about hardware engineering in a hands-on way, and to inspire them to solve problems in their own lives and across different fields with technology.

RB Ronith BalusaniFounder & Lead Instructor · teaching hardware since 2021
🧩

Struggle is the lesson

A circuit that does not work is not a failure of the lesson plan, it is the lesson. Our instructors are told to guide rather than solve, even when solving would be faster for everyone in the room.

🎁

The moment it works is the point

Every student designs and wires something themselves, and gets it working. That moment does more for a student's sense of what they can do than any amount of explanation.
