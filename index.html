Most students never touch the hardware side of STEM.

School computing has largely settled on software: simple block, then Python, then maybe a robotics club with a fixed kit. But there’s a whole other side of computing that students rarely get to experience! The physical layer underneath, where a sensor reading triggers a decision and becomes a moving part, is where a lot of students would actually catch fire. It’s also the part that needs equipment, a curriculum, and someone in the room who has wired a circuit before. That’s why we’re here.

  • Equipment is the first wall

    Students dont often have access to microcontrollers, breadboards, sensors, and motors

  • Then there’s the knowledge gap

    Most computer science certifications do not cover the hardware side of computing, so students often don’t have the time to learn the material on their own

  • So most students never get the chance

    Students decide what they are good at long before they choose a major. If nobody puts a breadboard in front of a student, they're unlikely to know whether they would enjoy working with one. We want to give them that chance.

It takes less than you might think.

A room, some outlets, and a group of students is close to the whole of it. We deliberately kept it that way. The Program page walks through the rest.

  • Format 3 sessions, 10 hours total
  • Grades 6 to 12, no prior experience needed
  • You bring A room with outlets, computers to code on, and an adult in the room
  • We bring Every kit, the curriculum, and the instructors
  • Cost Nothing.
