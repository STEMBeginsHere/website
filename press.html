Short (about 50 words)

STEMBeginsHere is a youth-led Arizona nonprofit corporation that runs free, hands-on hardware engineering workshops for students in grades 6 through 12. Students learn to code by building things that sense, decide, and move, with kits, curriculum, and instructors provided at no cost to schools or families. Learn more at stembeginshere.org.

Long (about 110 words)

STEMBeginsHere is a youth-led Arizona nonprofit corporation that runs free, hands-on hardware engineering workshops for students in grades 6 through 12. Students learn to code by building things that sense, decide, and move, with kits, curriculum, and instructors provided at no cost to schools or families. Founder Ronith Balusani began teaching Raspberry Pi to his classmates in 2021. Today the program runs one-day and two-day workshops at schools and community spaces, where students learn microcontrollers, breadboards, sensors, and motors, then design, wire, and present a project of their own. STEMBeginsHere is also launching a program to train organizations and individuals to start their own clubs. Learn more at stembeginshere.org.

Story angles

What makes this worth covering.

01

Student-founded, student-taught

A program started by a student who taught his own grade Raspberry Pi in 2021, now running workshops for schools.

02

The missing half of STEM

Most school computing stops at software. Hardware (sensors, circuits, motors) needs equipment and expertise most schools don't have.

03

Completely free

No cost to schools, students, or families. Kits, curriculum, and instructors all come with the program.
