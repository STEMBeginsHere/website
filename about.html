Built by people who wished this had existed.

STEM Begins Here is an Arizona nonprofit that puts real hardware in front of students in grades 6 to 12, because the gap between wanting to build something and knowing how to start is mostly a matter of access.

Founding Story

"I started creating hardware engineering projects at 8 years old, but I found it very frustrating that there were no easy ways to access courses which gave a strong breakdown of the fundamentals of hardware engineering. I wanted to create a program that would give students the opportunity to learn about hardware engineering in a hands-on way, and to inspire them to solve problems in their own lives and accross different fields with technology." - Ronith Balusani, Founder

Three convictions that shape everything we run.

Access is the bottleneck

Interest in building things is close to evenly distributed. Breadboards, sensors, and someone who can debug a circuit are not. We move the equipment and the expertise, not the students.

Struggle is the lesson

A circuit that does not work is not a failure of the lesson plan, it is the lesson. Our instructors are told to guide rather than solve, even when solving would be faster for everyone in the room.

They should leave with the thing

Every student finishes holding something they designed and wired themselves. That artifact does more for a student's sense of what they can do than any amount of explanation.

Who's behind it.

A small, founder-led team, with a board of directors behind it.
