What actually happens in the room.

Three sessions, ten hours, and a kit for every student. Here is the whole thing: what gets taught, how we teach it, what we bring, and what you would need on hand.

Who brings what.

We take care of the parts that need equipment and know-how. You take care of the room and the calendar. That is genuinely most of it.

We bring

  • A Raspberry Pi Pico kit for every student, including breadboard, sensors, motors, and wiring
  • The full curriculum and all teaching material
  • Instructors who have built this hardware themselves
  • Everything needed for the day three showcase and awards

You bring

  • A room with tables to spread out on, and outlets we can reach
  • Computers or laptops for the students, since code gets written on a regular machine and then moved onto the Pico
  • Someone from your side in the room with us
  • Three blocks of time that fit your calendar
  • A way to let students know it is happening

Still working this part out: how many students we can take at once, how many instructors we send, and our background checks and insurance. If there are boxes we need to tick before we can be in a room with your students, just tell us early and we will get them sorted.

Nobody gets handed the answer.

This is the part of the curriculum we are strictest about, and it is written into the instructor notes on every single day.

Questions, constantly

Instructors ask rather than tell, and keep asking until the class produces the definition themselves. It is slower on paper and considerably faster in practice.

Guide, do not solve

When a student is stuck, the instruction to our team is explicit: walk them to the answer using what they already know. Fixing the circuit for them ends the problem and the learning at the same time.

Nobody left behind quietly

Small groups and constant check-ins exist so that the student who stopped understanding twenty minutes ago gets noticed, rather than waiting it out.
