Three sessions, ten hours total. Day one covers the fundamentals and gets hands on a Raspberry Pi Pico, a breadboard, and a set of sensors and motors. Day two is an open build session where students design and wire their own project. Day three is a showcase where every student demonstrates what they made and explains how it works. The Program page has the hour-by-hour breakdown.

Grades 6 through 12, and no experience at all. The curriculum opens by rebuilding the definition of a computer from scratch, so a student who has never written a line of code and a student who has been coding for years both have somewhere to go.

Nothing. STEM Begins Here is self-funded and covers the kits, the curriculum, and the instructors. Nobody pays anything, not the school and not the students or their families.

A room with tables and outlets, computers the students can write code on, someone from your side in the room, and three blocks of time. The computers are the one thing worth checking early: code gets written on a regular machine and then moved onto the microcontroller. If that is tricky where you are, say so and we will figure something out.

It depends on how many kits and instructors we can get to your dates, so we work the number out with you rather than promising one we might not hit. Students work in small groups with instructors moving between them the whole time, and we always want an adult from your end in the room too. If there are background checks or paperwork we need to clear first, mention it when you write and we will get on it.
